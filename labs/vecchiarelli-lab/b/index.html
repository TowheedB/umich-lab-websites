01 — About

The Vecchiarelli Lab studies how bacterial cells organize their organelles and other components at the subcellular level, challenging the outdated view of bacteria as disorganized sacks of molecules. Rather than cytoskeletons and motor proteins, bacteria use ParA/MinD-family ATPases to position protein-based organelles. A central model is the carboxysome, the Rubisco-containing compartment responsible for almost half of global CO2 fixation, whose positioning by the McdA/McdB system the lab has characterized. The lab also asks whether bacteria form membraneless biomolecular condensates that compartmentalize biochemistry, and how multiple ParA/MinD ATPases coordinate the trafficking of disparate cargos in the same cell, using organisms such as Synechococcus elongatus and Halothiobacillus neapolitanus. The group combines genetics, cell biology, biochemistry, quantitative microscopy, and bioinformatics.

02 — Research themes
01Organelle trafficking and homeostasisSpatial regulation of bacterial organelles, especially carboxysomes, which contain Rubisco and are responsible for almost half of global CO2 fixation. The lab investigates how cells position these organelles via the McdA/McdB system.
02Material state of bacterial organellesWhether bacteria form membraneless compartments (biomolecular condensates) like those found in eukaryotic cells, and whether such phase-separated structures compartmentalize biochemical reactions.
03Coordinated organelle traffickingHow multiple ParA/MinD ATPases coordinate the positioning of diverse cellular components in a single cell, using Halothiobacillus neapolitanus, which encodes seven such ATPases, to understand how trafficking is synchronized with the cell cycle.
