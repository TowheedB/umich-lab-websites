01 — About

The Aton Lab studies how neuronal and network activity during sleep and waking contributes to neural plasticity following novel sensory experiences. Using mice, the lab investigates how sleep supports two processes: hippocampal long-term memory formation and visual cortex plasticity. The group combines behavioral, biochemical, electrophysiological, and optogenetic techniques in freely behaving animals to link brain-state-specific network activity to the intracellular signaling and structural changes that consolidate memories. The lab also asks whether abnormal sleep contributes to cognitive deficits in neurodevelopmental disorders such as Fragile X syndrome, Angelman syndrome, and autism spectrum disorders.

02 — Research themes
01Thalamocortical oscillations and sleep-dependent plasticityTests whether high-amplitude, low-frequency oscillations during sleep uniquely promote cortical plasticity, using optogenetic tools to silence oscillation-generating neurons while recording neural activity and plasticity in freely behaving mice.
02Sleep and hippocampal memory consolidationExplores how network activity during sleep activates intracellular signaling pathways needed to convert short-term memories into long-term memories, combining electrophysiology, optogenetics, and biochemistry.
03Sleep and neurodevelopmental disordersInvestigates whether abnormal sleep contributes to cognitive deficits in autism spectrum disorders, Fragile X syndrome, and Angelman syndrome, and how disease-associated mutations affect sleep behavior and the network activity associated with memory consolidation.
