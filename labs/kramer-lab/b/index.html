About

What drives the lab

The Kramer Lab studies cellular and synaptic neurobiology in pursuit of new knowledge that will advance human health. Its overarching hypothesis is that axons have an active and unexplored role in neurotransmission: axons can compute information and generate output that contributes to behavior and to the etiology of neurological disease, much like the somatodendritic compartment. The lab focuses on dopaminergic axons in the striatum, how cholinergic and GABAergic inputs modulate them through axo-axonic transmission, and how this signaling is altered in substance use disorders (especially nicotine use disorder) and Parkinson's disease. In 2026 the lab received a NIDA-supported R01 to study the cellular mechanisms of nicotine use disorder.

Research

Where we focus

01

Axon physiology

The lab's central focus: how axons actively participate in neurotransmission and information processing, challenging the traditional view that limits neuronal computation to the cell body and dendrites.

02

Axo-axonic transmission

Investigates communication between axons and neighboring axonal segments, including synaptic-like transmission from striatal cholinergic interneurons onto dopaminergic fibers.

03

Substance use disorders

Examines how drugs of abuse, including nicotine, alter neuronal communication within brain reward circuitry.

04

Parkinson's disease

Studies how disrupted dopaminergic neuronal signaling contributes to this neurodegenerative condition.
