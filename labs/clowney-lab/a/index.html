Research themes

All research →
01

Sex-specific mating circuits

Examines how male fruit flies instinctively identify mates and initiate courtship, focusing on how the male-specific transcription factor Fruitless generates sex-differentiated neural circuits through molecular dissection, imaging, genetics, circuit mapping, and behavior.

02

Randomized wiring in the mushroom body

Investigates general-purpose learning circuits that process evolutionarily unpredicted sensory information, using connectomics, developmental imaging, and transcriptional approaches to test whether a simple developmental algorithm generates learning capacity through non-deterministic patterning.

03

Engineering development for olfactory coding

Tests theoretical predictions about circuit function by experimentally perturbing neural circuit development during embryogenesis and measuring the resulting changes in sensory coding and behavior.

04

Vertebrate genome organization and transcriptional regulation

Analyzes how chromosomal gene organization relates to regulatory control, examining how AT/GC sequence content correlates with genomic spacing and gene evolution in humans.

About the lab

The Clowney Lab investigates how neural circuits that drive survival behaviors (feeding, fighting, fleeing, mating, parenting, and sleeping) are organized in the brain and how that organization is produced during development. Using developmental biology, connectomics, imaging, genetics, and behavioral analysis, the lab studies the DNA-encoded recipes that build neurons and wire them into functional circuits, focusing on chemosensory circuits in the fruit fly. Projects examine how the male-specific transcription factor Fruitless generates sex-differentiated mating circuits, how randomized wiring in the mushroom body supports general-purpose learning, and how perturbing circuit development changes sensory coding and behavior. The lab also studies vertebrate genome organization, including how AT and GC sequence content relate to gene spacing and the evolution of neurodevelopmental genes.
