Research

What we study

Exploring how nutrients reshape the brain and influence hunger, physiology, and behavior.

Overview

The Dus Laboratory of Molecular Nutrition views nutrients as more than energy and raw materials: they act as life's sensors, regulators, and actuators. The lab works across molecules, genes, cells, organs, and whole organisms to understand how nutrients guide biology, health, and disease. Its initial focus on how diet affects the brain has expanded to developmental and aging processes. Using Drosophila and rodent models, the lab has shown that sugar breakdown products silence genes required for taste cell function, that high-sugar diets desensitize brain circuits controlling appetite and food memory, and that these effects can be reversed by returning to lower-sugar diets. Current work addresses questions in precision nutrition, including optimal diets across life stages and the role of genetic variation.

Research themes

5 active directions

  1. Nutrigenomics: how nutrients manipulate genetic switches

    The lab discovered that nutrients and their metabolic byproducts can activate or silence genes, with particular impacts on brain function and health.

  2. Sugar's impact on taste perception

    Sugar breakdown products silence genes essential for taste cell function, causing animals to lose sweetness sensitivity with increased consumption, which in turn alters food choices.

  3. Sugar's effects on brain regulation of eating

    High sugar consumption desensitizes brain regions controlling appetite and disrupts dopamine signaling needed to form food memories important for satiety.

  4. Reversibility of dietary damage

    Negative effects of high-sugar diets can be reversed by returning to lower-sugar options, suggesting taste buds and brains can be retrained toward healthier eating.

  5. Precision nutrition

    Current investigations address optimal diets for different life stages, genetic factors in nutrition, and whether truly personalized nutrition is achievable.
