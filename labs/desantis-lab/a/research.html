Research

What we study

Revealing how molecular motors move essential cargo through the cell.

Overview

The DeSantis lab studies microtubule-associated motor proteins, which transport cellular cargo including organelles, mRNA, viruses, and protein aggregates. The lab focuses on cytoplasmic dynein, a 1.5 MDa motor complex that moves cargo toward the cell interior. Researchers ask how dynein is assembled in living cells, how binding proteins such as Lis1, Ndel1, and cargo adaptors modify its activity, how it binds and transports specific cargoes, how it is correctly localized within cells, and how its dysfunction contributes to disease. The group combines single-molecule TIRF microscopy, protein biochemistry, cryo-electron microscopy, and live-cell imaging.

Research themes

3 active directions

  1. Dynein motor assembly and activation

    The lab investigates how the 1.5 MDa dynein complex is assembled in living cells and how regulators such as Lis1, Ndel1, and cargo adaptors control the mechanism and kinetics of dynein activation.

  2. Cargo recognition and transport

    Researchers study how dynein binds and transports specific cargoes, including organelles, mRNA, viruses (e.g., HIV-1), and protein aggregates, and how adaptor identity dictates transport.

  3. Dynein localization and disease

    The group examines how dynein is properly localized within cells (e.g., gating at the cell periphery by CCSer2) and how impaired motor function is associated with neurodevelopmental and other diseases.
