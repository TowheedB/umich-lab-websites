Research

What we study

Mapping the neural circuits that allow the nervous system to sense pain, itch, and touch.

Overview

The Bo Duan lab studies how the nervous system perceives and processes somatosensory information, including temperature, touch, pain, and itch. The lab investigates the neural circuits that transmit and process these distinct sensory modalities in the spinal cord and brain, and how the modalities interact with one another. Current projects aim to identify the spinal neurons that transmit thermal versus mechanical pain, the spinal circuits for mechanical itch, the peripheral sensory inputs that drive pain and itch, and the descending pathways that modulate pain and itch. The lab combines mouse genetics, histochemistry, neuroanatomical tracing, electrophysiology, in vivo imaging, and behavioral analyses.

Research themes

4 active directions

  1. Thermal vs. mechanical pain transmission

    Identifying the spinal neurons responsible for transmitting different types of pain.

  2. Mechanical itch circuits

    Identifying the spinal circuits and dedicated hair afferents that transmit mechanical itch signals.

  3. Peripheral inputs for pain and itch

    Characterizing the peripheral sensory inputs that drive painful and itchy sensations, including dedicated skin-to-brain circuits for temperature sensation.

  4. Descending modulation of pain and itch

    Dissecting the descending neural pathways that regulate pain and itch responses.
