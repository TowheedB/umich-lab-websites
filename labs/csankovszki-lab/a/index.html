About

What drives the lab

The Csankovszki lab studies three basic processes of developmental biology in the nematode C. elegans: regulation of gene expression, segregation of chromosomes, and cytokinesis. These seemingly distinct processes are connected by condensin complexes. C. elegans has three condensins: Condensin IDC regulates X chromosome gene expression during dosage compensation, while Condensins I and II control mitotic and meiotic chromosome segregation. The lab investigates how condensin IDC binds both X chromosomes in hermaphrodites and halves their expression through chromosome compaction, histone modifications (H4K20me1 enrichment, H4K16ac depletion), and tethering to the nuclear lamina. It also examines how condensins I and II function in mitosis and meiosis, including maintenance of the cytokinetic furrow and regulation of cohesin levels, with implications for oocyte health during aging.

Research

Where we focus

01

Condensin IDC and regulation of X-linked gene expression

In C. elegans dosage compensation, condensin IDC binds both X chromosomes in hermaphrodites to downregulate expression from both by half. The lab examines how chromosome compaction, histone modifications (H4K20me1 enrichment and H4K16ac depletion), and nuclear lamina tethering work together to achieve X chromosome repression.

02

Condensins I and II in chromosome segregation and cell division

The lab studies how condensin I and II operate during mitosis and meiosis, including condensin I's role in maintaining the cytokinetic furrow during chromosome segregation errors and its regulation of cohesin levels during meiosis by antagonizing the anti-cohesion factor WAPL-1. Condensin function may contribute to oocyte health during aging.
