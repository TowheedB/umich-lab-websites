Research

What we study

Revealing how cells preserve tissue barriers while dividing and responding to mechanical force.

Overview

The Miller Lab studies how epithelial cell-cell junctions maintain adhesion and barrier function while being dynamically remodeled during cell shape changes such as cytokinesis and morphogenesis. The lab uses Xenopus laevis embryos with live-cell imaging and quantitative microscopy to examine Rho GTPase regulation in space and time, actin cytoskeletal dynamics, and the roles of proteins including Anillin, MgcRacGAP, and Vinculin. Key discoveries include Rho flares, localized bursts of Rho activity that repair leaks in tight junctions, visualized with the lab's ZnUMBA barrier assay, and mechanosensitive recruitment of Vinculin to tricellular junctions. The lab also studies cortical excitability and cytokinesis using reconstituted systems of supported lipid bilayers and Xenopus egg extracts.

Research themes

5 active directions

  1. Rho flares and tight junction repair

    Localized Rho activation restores barrier function after junction breaches by concentrating tight junction proteins through actin polymerization and ROCK-mediated contraction, visualized with the lab's Zinc-based Ultrasensitive Microscopic Barrier Assay (ZnUMBA).

  2. Cell-cell junction remodeling during epithelial cytokinesis

    Investigates how adhesion and barrier function persist as epithelial cells divide, including Vinculin-mediated stabilization of adherens junction proteins at the cleavage furrow and how neighbor cells restrain furrowing.

  3. Anillin in junctional and actomyosin regulation

    The scaffolding protein Anillin regulates both junction integrity and cell mechanics; depletion reduces junctional F-actin while overexpression causes hypercontractility.

  4. Cortical pattern formation and excitability

    Studies sustained waves of Rho activity and actin assembly using reconstituted supported lipid bilayers combined with Xenopus egg extracts imaged by TIRF microscopy.

  5. Cytokinesis regulation by Rho GTPase flux

    Examines how RhoA activity zones form and focus the contractile ring during cell division, with emphasis on the regulatory role of MgcRacGAP.
