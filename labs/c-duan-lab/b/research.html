Research

What we study

Understanding how growth-factor signaling coordinates growth, development, and aging.

Overview

The Duan lab investigates the molecular mechanisms governing cell cycle plasticity during tissue formation, growth, and regeneration. Using a zebrafish model in which differentiated epithelial cells can be induced to re-enter the cell cycle in response to stress, the lab asks how cells decide between quiescence and proliferation when faced with changing nutrient availability, oxygen levels, and pH. The group's approaches include CRISPR/Cas9 gene editing, in vivo imaging, chemical biology, and RNA-seq. Many of the regulators the lab has discovered converge on the insulin/insulin-like growth factor (IIS)-PI3 kinase-Akt-TOR signaling pathway. The lab also studies hypoxia and HIF biology, stanniocalcins, and calcium homeostasis.

Research themes

2 active directions

  1. Tissue formation, growth, and regeneration

    The lab examines how the insulin/IGF (IIS)-PI3K-Akt-TOR signaling pathway regulates cell cycle plasticity when cells encounter varying nutrient, oxygen, and pH conditions during tissue maintenance and healing, using a zebrafish epithelial cell reactivation model.

  2. Hypoxia and HIF biology

    The lab studies how hypoxia signaling, including stanniocalcins, shapes IGF-stimulated growth versus organismal survival under hypoxic stress.
