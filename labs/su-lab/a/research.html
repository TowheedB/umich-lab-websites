Research

What we study

Mapping the neural circuits that allow the brain to sense the lungs and regulate physiology.

Overview

The Su Lab (Body-Brain Neural Circuits Lab) studies body-brain communication in health and disease, using the lung-brain axis as a model for interoception. The lab asks how signals from the lung, including allergens, inflammation, toxins, and environmental exposures, are detected, transmitted through defined brainstem and higher brain pathways, and integrated in the central nervous system with cell-type specificity. It also investigates top-down neural circuits that regulate airway physiology and systemic responses, and how lung-brain circuits are remodeled in pulmonary and neurological disease. Approaches include neural circuit mapping, single-cell profiling, opto- and chemogenetics, electrophysiology, whole-animal physiology, and behavioral analysis, with the long-term goal of identifying targets for neuromodulation therapies.

Research themes

3 active directions

  1. Lung-to-brain communication

    How allergens, inflammation, toxins, and environmental exposures are sensed in the lung and relayed to the brain through defined brainstem and higher-order pathways with cell-type specificity.

  2. Central control of lung function

    Top-down neural circuits that regulate airway physiology and systemic responses, with a focus on identifying molecular and functional specificity that could enable precise neuromodulation therapies.

  3. Circuit remodeling in disease

    How lung-brain neural circuits become dysregulated in pulmonary and neurological diseases, and how perturbations reshape circuit activity and drive plasticity.
