Research

What we study

Decoding how Wnt signaling controls gene expression during development.

Overview

The Cadigan Lab investigates signal transduction by Wnts, a class of secreted glycoproteins that regulate cell fate decisions during development and that are misregulated in cancers. The lab uses Drosophila genetics to study Wingless (Wg) signaling, combining genetic screens for pathway components with promoter analysis, biochemistry, and genomics to understand how the transcription factor TCF and beta-catenin activate or repress target genes. Recent work has examined beta-catenin biomolecular condensates, TCF binding sites and co-regulators such as SOX9, CDX, and TLE, and extends to Wnt target gene regulation in colorectal cancer cells. The lab also identified a protein kinase that regulates normal apoptosis levels in fly embryos, establishing a secondary line of research on programmed cell death.

Research themes

3 active directions

  1. Wnt/Wingless signaling mechanisms

    Dissects how Wingless signaling activates transcription in Drosophila, including the roles of Pygopus, which acts downstream of TCF and is required for Wg signaling in all tissues examined, and its vertebrate homologs in human cells.

  2. Transcriptional regulation of Wnt target genes

    Studies how TCF transcription factors recognize DNA, how beta-catenin condensates and co-factors such as SOX9, CDX, and TLE switch target genes between activation and repression, and how these mechanisms operate in colorectal cancer.

  3. Apoptosis regulation

    Genetic screening identified a protein kinase that regulates normal levels of programmed cell death in fruit fly embryos, a secondary research direction in the lab.
