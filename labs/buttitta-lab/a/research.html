Research

What we study

Discovering how cells control proliferation and how those safeguards break down in disease.

Overview

The Buttitta Lab studies states of G0 in development, cancer, and aging: how the cell cycle machinery is controlled during the decision to exit the cell cycle, and how quiescence is made reversible or irreversible in different biological contexts. The lab uses Drosophila melanogaster genetics together with mammalian cell lines to dissect the signals, phosphatases, and chromatin changes that enforce cell cycle exit. Projects range from the PP2A-B56 phosphatase complex and tumor dormancy in prostate cancer, to chromatin-mediated silencing of cell cycle genes in postmitotic tissues, hormone-driven quiescence and polyploid cell cycles in the fly accessory gland, and abnormal cell cycle re-entry in the aging brain. The work has applications to tissue regeneration, aging, and cancer.

Research themes

4 active directions

  1. Modulators of the proliferation-quiescence decision

    Studies how the PP2A phosphatase complex, particularly the B56 regulatory subunit, promotes cell cycle exit by dephosphorylating Cdk2, extending the work to mammalian cells with fluorescent cell cycle sensors and to tumor dormancy in prostate cancer models.

  2. Silencing cell cycle genes: chromatin changes during G0

    Uses FAIRE-seq, ATAC-seq, and RNA-seq in fruit fly wings to show how nucleosome-mediated silencing and enhancer decommissioning shut down genes such as Cyclin E and cdc25c in prolonged quiescence, with ongoing work on the Brahma and NuRD complexes.

  3. Developmental signals that promote quiescence

    Examines how the steroid hormone ecdysone triggers cell cycle arrest during metamorphosis, and studies polyploid and variant cell cycles in the Drosophila accessory gland, a prostate-like organ.

  4. Cell cycle exit in the aged brain

    Investigates abnormal cell cycle re-entry in aging neurons using the fruit fly brain, developing genetic tools to force re-entry and assessing neurodegeneration with behavioral assays.
