About the lab

The Stockbridge Lab studies membrane transport proteins, the channels and energy-coupled pumps that serve as molecular gatekeepers of the cell. The group works to understand the structure, dynamics, and function of these proteins, with particular interest in how bacteria resist environmental stressors such as antiseptics, PFAS, pharmaceuticals, and pollutants. Major projects include fluoride export channels in oral pathogens and eukaryotes, the small multidrug resistance (SMR) family of exporters that move antiseptics and pharmaceutical metabolites such as metformin breakdown products, and the molecular evolution of new protein folds and functions. The lab combines cryo-EM, X-ray crystallography, biochemistry, single-molecule biophysics, microbiology, and evolutionary analysis, and is supported by NIH and NSF.

Supported by NIH, NSF, Alexander von Humboldt Foundation.

Research themes

All research →
01

Structural biology of membrane proteins

Cryo-EM and X-ray crystallography of small membrane transport proteins, including development of epitope tags that make these small proteins tractable for structure determination.

02

Fluoride resistance in oral pathogens

How fluoride channels (Fluc, FEX) export fluoride, how fluoride triggers lysis in Streptococcus mutans, and whether fluoride export is required for pathogen fitness in dental biofilm models.

03

Bacterial resistance to antiseptics and pharmaceutical metabolites

Mechanisms by which small multidrug resistance (SMR) transporters such as EmrE and guanidinium exporters move antiseptics and metformin metabolites across the membrane, including the residues that encode transport specificity.

04

Molecular evolution of transporters

How novel protein folds and transport functions arise, studied through evolutionary analysis combined with biophysical characterization.
