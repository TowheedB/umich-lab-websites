Research

What we study

Understanding how neuromuscular synapses develop, adapt, and remain stable in health and disease.

Overview

The Akaaboune Lab investigates the cellular and molecular mechanisms underlying the dynamics and trafficking of postsynaptic proteins at healthy and compromised synapses. The lab uses the neuromuscular junction as its primary model because it is the simplest and best-characterized synapse in vertebrates. The team combines advanced in vivo imaging that follows the same synapses over extended periods with molecular and biochemical approaches in living mice. Current work addresses how high-fat diet, aging, and loss of dystrophin glycoprotein complex components alter the stability of nicotinic acetylcholine receptors and scaffold proteins such as rapsyn and alpha-kap at the postsynaptic membrane.

  • Synaptic development and maintenance
  • neuromuscular junctions
  • diet and neuromuscular disease

Research themes

5 active directions

  1. High-fat diet and neuromuscular junction stability

    Examines how obesity induced by a high-fat diet affects the structural integrity and disassembly of the neuromuscular junction in mouse models.

  2. Dystrophin glycoprotein complex function

    Studies the role of the dystrophin glycoprotein complex (including alpha-dystrobrevin and alpha-syntrophin) in maintaining nicotinic acetylcholine receptor stability at synapses.

  3. Aging and the postsynaptic apparatus

    Investigates how aging alters the stability and organization of postsynaptic components at the neuromuscular junction.

  4. Molecular interactions in living systems

    Analyzes spatial and temporal interactions between postsynaptic molecular components using live imaging in mice.

  5. Rapsyn function in non-muscle contexts

    Explores the role of the scaffold protein rapsyn in cellular environments outside muscle, including lysosome clustering.
