About the lab

The Kumar Lab applies functional genomics, proteomics, genetics, cell biology, and biochemistry to probe central signaling pathways underlying eukaryotic cell growth and function. A major focus is the transition of yeast cells from unicellular to filamentous (pseudohyphal) growth, a nutrient-responsive developmental program related to the virulence of pathogenic fungi. The lab studies ERK/MAPK and AMP-activated kinase (Snf1) signaling, uses quantitative phosphoproteomics by mass spectrometry to map kinase networks, examines the regulation of P-bodies and stress granules, and investigates metabolic messengers such as inositol polyphosphates in developmental contexts. Work is carried out in Saccharomyces cerevisiae and the human fungal pathogen Candida albicans, including studies of Candida biofilm rheology.

Research themes

All research →
01

Proteomic analysis of kinase signaling

Studies how yeast cells respond to nutritional and environmental stress through protein kinase signaling, including how the kinase Sks1p integrates nitrogen and glucose stress signals, and the relevance of these networks to Candida albicans.

02

Metabolic signaling in yeast pseudohyphal growth

Examines how the Snf1p/AMP-activated kinase regulates morphological transitions and energy homeostasis; the lab showed that Snf1p phosphorylates the inositol polyphosphate kinase Kcs1p, linking nutrient availability to cell growth.

03

Amyloid biology and yeast filamentous growth

Explores functional amyloid structures in fungal adhesins, finding that nearly 90 percent of identified adhesins contain sequences with high amyloid-forming potential, and developed a Flo11-based surface display system for studying amyloid aggregation.

04

Large-scale analysis of the yeast kinome during filamentous growth

A systematic screen of yeast kinases revealed five kinases that relocalize to the nucleus during filamentous growth, forming an interdependent, stress-responsive regulatory network.
