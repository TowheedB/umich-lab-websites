About the lab

The Kargbo-Hill Lab studies how neurons, as non-dividing cells that must survive for a lifetime, regulate gene expression to adapt to changing and sometimes stressful conditions. The lab focuses on two mechanisms, RNA splicing and active DNA demethylation, and how they are specialized in neurons and altered during aging and neurodegeneration. A central theme is the RNA-binding protein TDP-43, whose nuclear loss drives cryptic exon inclusion in ALS, frontotemporal dementia, and Alzheimer's disease. The lab combines human iPSC-derived neurons, the nematode C. elegans, live imaging, and multi-omics (epigenomic, transcriptomic, splicing, and proteomic) approaches.

Research themes

All research →
01

TDP-43 and RNA splicing in neurodegeneration

Investigates how TDP-43 regulates splicing and how its translocation from nucleus to cytosol in ALS, FTD, and Alzheimer's disease leads to cryptic exon inclusion that reduces target gene expression. Work in human iPSC-derived neurons identified novel pathologic splicing in the ALS/TDP-associated synaptic gene UNC13A.

02

RNA splicing in C. elegans

Uses the nematode's ubiquitously expressed TDP-43 ortholog, TDP-1, to explore tissue-specific mechanisms of cryptic splicing regulation, taking advantage of the worm's optical accessibility and conserved RNA metabolism for studies of neuronal aging.

03

Neuronal DNA (de)methylation

Examines active DNA demethylation as a gene regulatory mechanism in non-dividing neurons, building on the discovery that neurons undergo widespread DNA synthesis at enhancers to repair recurrent single-stranded DNA breaks, and asks how this is used during acute stress responses.
