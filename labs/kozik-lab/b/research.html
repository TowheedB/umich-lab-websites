Research

What we study

Deciphering the molecular conversations between microbes and the human respiratory tract.

Overview

The Kozik Lab investigates host-microbe and microbe-microbe crosstalk in the human respiratory tract, with a focus on how immune-microbiome interactions shape chronic respiratory disease, particularly asthma. The lab uses multi-omic approaches spanning the microbiome, genome, transcriptome, metabolome, and proteome to understand dynamic interactions in the airways and asthma heterogeneity. A major experimental focus is the genus Prevotella, including how the lung symbiont Prevotella melaninogenica tolerates oxygen, how it can be made genetically and experimentally tractable, and whether lung commensals metabolize inhaled corticosteroids. The lab also works to advance precision medicine by measuring lived environmental conditions rather than relying on broad population labels. Its stated values are teamwork, communication, curiosity, and community, with a commitment that science belongs to everyone.

Research themes

4 active directions

  1. The Prevotella paradox and oxygen adaptation

    Asks how Prevotella melaninogenica, classified as a strict anaerobe for over a century, thrives in the oxygenated respiratory tract. The lab's preprinted work shows active growth in hypoxic conditions and gene expression shifts suggesting oxygen tolerance, and it is identifying mechanisms of oxygen detection and neutralization and their effects on microbe-microbe competition and persistence during airway inflammation.

  2. Expanding experimental tractability for Prevotella

    Develops laboratory protocols and genetic tools so that Prevotella can be studied functionally in controlled settings, moving beyond observational genomic correlations.

  3. Microbial biotransformation of inhaled corticosteroids

    Explores whether lung commensals, especially Prevotella species, carry biochemical machinery that metabolically alters inhaled corticosteroids, potentially explaining partial or complete nonresponsiveness in some patients.

  4. The ecosocial interface

    Works to advance precision medicine in microbiome research by shifting from broad population labels toward direct measurement of the lived environmental conditions that influence health outcomes.
