Research

What we study

Uncovering how non-coding RNA and chromatin work together to control genome activity.

Overview

The Wierzbicki Lab studies how gene expression is regulated through chromatin structure and non-coding RNAs in plants. One major area is chloroplast chromatin: how chloroplast DNA is organized and packaged by nucleoid-associated proteins and how this packaging, including membrane association of active genes, connects to transcription by the plastid-encoded RNA polymerase. The second area is RNA-mediated transcriptional silencing, in which small interfering RNAs and long non-coding RNAs produced by the plant-specific polymerases Pol IV and Pol V direct DNA methylation, nucleosome positioning, and chromatin modifications that silence transposons and regulate genes. The lab works primarily in Arabidopsis thaliana, with additional work in maize, cucumber, and tomato, and combines mutant analysis, protein-RNA-DNA interaction assays, chromatin profiling, and high-throughput sequencing with bioinformatic analysis.

Research themes

2 active directions

  1. Chloroplast chromatin

    How chloroplast DNA is organized and packaged by nucleoid-associated proteins and how this packaging is connected to gene expression, studied with genetic tools in Arabidopsis, maize, cucumber, and tomato as the chloroplast equivalent of chromatin.

  2. RNA-mediated transcriptional silencing

    How siRNAs and long non-coding RNAs cooperate to silence transposons and regulate genes through chromatin modifications, including how lncRNA production is regulated, how chromatin-modifying enzymes are recruited, and how nucleosome positioning and chromosome organization are controlled.
