Research

What we study

Discovering how plant cells build and organize the walls that shape their growth.

Overview

The Nielsen Lab investigates plant cell wall biosynthesis in Arabidopsis thaliana, exploring the cell biology underlying this essential process, which is crucial for plant growth, development, and defense. The lab uses genetic, biochemical, and live-cell imaging techniques to study the cellular and molecular mechanisms of cell wall formation, with emphasis on how cellulose synthase (CESA) and cellulose synthase-like D (CSLD) proteins are sorted and delivered to specific plasma membrane domains during polarized growth. Additional work examines KORRIGAN accessory proteins that regulate cellulose synthase activity and how plants sense and respond to artificial nanomaterials derived from cell wall fractions.

  • Plant cell walls
  • membrane trafficking
  • cell-wall deposition

Research themes

3 active directions

  1. Cellulose synthesis protein sorting

    Identifies how CESA and CSLD proteins are delivered to specific plasma membrane domains, including CSLD3 as a beta-1,4-glucan synthase in tip-growing cells and CSLD5 in cell plate formation.

  2. KORRIGAN proteins

    Investigates accessory proteins that regulate cellulose synthase activity during cell wall synthesis.

  3. Plant responses to nanomaterials

    Studies how plants sense and respond to artificial nanomaterials derived from cell wall fractions, in the context of agricultural nanotechnology.
