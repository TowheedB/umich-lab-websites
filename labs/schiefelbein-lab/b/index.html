Research themes

All research →
01

Single-cell RNA sequencing in plants

The lab has adapted scRNA-seq to individual plant cells, revealing gene expression from single Arabidopsis root cells and providing insight into cellular heterogeneity, rare cell types, and developmental trajectories. Work continues on expanding applications of this method in plants.

02

Root and root-hair development programs across the plant kingdom

Using available plant genome sequences, the lab performs comparative transcript profiling of developing roots and root hairs across diverse plant species to identify conserved and divergent gene expression programs.

03

Cell-type pattern formation in the root epidermis

The lab investigates the molecular mechanisms that determine root epidermal cell patterning in Arabidopsis and other species, including how regulatory proteins interact, when developmental mechanisms are activated, the evolutionary origins of the patterning system, and the downstream factors in the gene regulatory network.

About the lab

The Schiefelbein Lab seeks to understand the molecular mechanisms that enable distinct cell fates to arise during the development of multicellular organisms. The lab focuses on the specification and patterning of hair and non-hair cells in the root epidermis of Arabidopsis thaliana, a simple tissue containing only two cell types in which cells make a binary developmental decision based on their position relative to underlying cortical cells. Arabidopsis offers rapidly observable root development, a simple tissue structure, genetic tractability, and a fully sequenced genome. The lab uses molecular genetic approaches, including mutant analysis, tissue-specific transcript profiling, and single-cell RNA sequencing, and extends its comparative work on root and root-hair development across the plant kingdom.
